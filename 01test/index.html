Digital Citizenship

What is Digital Citizenship?

Digital citizenship refers to the responsible and ethical use of technology by anyone who uses computers, the internet, and digital devices to engage with society on any level.

In simpler terms, it's about being a good citizen online — knowing how to act safely, respectfully, and responsibly in digital spaces.

What is Digital Law?

Digital law refers to the rules and legal guidelines that govern how people use technology, the internet, and digital content.

What is AUP?

An Acceptable Use Policy (AUP) is a document that outlines what users can and cannot do when using a network, computer, or online service. It helps ensure that everyone uses digital tools safely, responsibly, and legally.

What is Plagiarism?

Plagiarism is presenting someone else's words, ideas, work, or creations as your own without proper attribution.

What is Academic Dishonesty?

Academic dishonesty is any intentional act that misrepresents one's work or undermines fair evaluation in an academic setting, primarily involving plagiarism, cheating, fabrication, and collusion, like stealing ideas, using unauthorized aids, inventing data, or helping others cheat, all violating principles of honesty and original work.
